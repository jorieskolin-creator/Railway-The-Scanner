Core Idea: The Three-Layer Accuracy Strategy

The scanner uses three independent intelligence layers to detect Reality Gaps — the difference between what a company says it is and what evidence shows it actually is.

Layer 1
Intent
What they claim
Layer 2
Reality
What evidence shows
Layer 3
Synthesis
Cross-verify & reconcile

Reality Gap = Intent Score − Reality Score.
Large gap + washing detected = high-risk signal (ambitious promises, weak operations).
Small gap = honest company (walks the walk).
Negative gap (reality > intent) = humble, under-communicating company.

Phase 1: Intent Analysis Scanner 1
1
Narrative Ambition Assessment
"How ambitious is this company's narrative compared to typical companies? What do they say they are?"

What It Analyzes

Reads the company's own words — website text, uploaded documents, reports — and scores their stated intentions across 5 dimensions defined in the Knowledge Base:

Operating Model Autonomy, flow, teams vs. command-and-control, silos
Strategy Adaptive, customer-centric vs. static, top-down
Culture & Values Psychological safety, learning vs. compliance, fear
Service Portfolio SaaS, API, platform vs. legacy project-based
AI Readiness Data platforms, ML adoption vs. spreadsheets

How It Thinks

  • Scans text for "modernization" keywords (positive signals) and "legacy" keywords (negative signals) per dimension
  • Applies negation detection: "we avoid waterfall" does NOT count as a negative signal for "waterfall"
  • Applies word-boundary matching: prevents false positives from partial word matches
  • Calculates net ambition per dimension: positive signals − (negative signals × friction weight)
  • Normalizes for text length (short documents aren't penalized, long documents don't inflate scores)
  • Computes a Friction Index (0–1) showing how mixed/contradictory the signals are

Key Outputs

  • Intent Score (0–100): Average ambition across all dimensions
  • Per-dimension breakdown: Individual scores + matched keywords
  • Friction Index: Degree of mixed signals (modern + legacy language together)
  • Vibrancy Score: Keyword density / richness of narrative

Accuracy Safeguards

  • Negation detection prevents false positives ("eliminate silos" is positive, not negative)
  • Word boundary checks prevent partial matches ("streams" is not "stream-aligned teams")
  • Short vs. long text normalization prevents length bias
  • High friction index flags contradictory messaging
Intent Score passed to Scanner 2 for washing detection
Phase 2: Reality Check Scanner 2
2
5-Jury Forensic Evidence Search
"What does independent, external evidence show about this company's actual operational maturity?"

The 5-Jury Model

Five independent, specialized "juries" each hunt for evidence in a different area. This ensures no single dimension dominates the assessment:

Jury 1: Architecture

GitHub repos, engineering blogs, tech stacks, job postings. Evidence of modern vs. legacy architecture.

Jury 2: Strategy

Annual reports, financial databases, procurement, investments. Are they investing in digital transformation?

Jury 3: Culture

Glassdoor reviews, LinkedIn hiring patterns, meetup sponsorships. Can they retain talent? Do people rejoin?

Jury 4: AI & Data

Hugging Face models, tech stacks, cloud case studies, R&D publications. Real ML adoption or "pilot purgatory"?

Jury 5: Service Portfolio

Service pages, case studies, analyst reports. Can they deliver modern services or just legacy consulting?

How It Thinks

  • Uses AI with live web search grounding — the model can search the internet in real-time for each jury
  • Starts at a neutral base score of 50, then applies evidence-based adjustments (−50 to +50)
  • Recency weighting: Evidence less than 12 months old has full impact; evidence older than 18 months has only 20% impact
  • Technical debt penalty: Recent evidence of legacy systems or manual workarounds applies −20 to −35 points
  • Near-zero evidence penalty: If almost no evidence found across all juries, applies −35 to −50 points
  • Searches in both English and Finnish to cover Nordic companies
  • For holding companies, searches subsidiary companies separately

Re-Search Loop (Confidence-Driven)

If initial search returns low confidence (<45) or very few evidence links (<3):

  • Identifies which juries came back empty (weak juries)
  • Builds targeted re-search with broader terms
  • Merges new findings with existing ones (deduplicated by URL)
  • Each new unique finding adds +5 confidence; each resolved jury adds +10
  • Repeats up to 2 times (single scan) or 1 time (batch scan)
  • Stops when confidence ≥45 or no new evidence found

Evidence Validation Gate

  • Every evidence URL is verified via HTTP request (does it actually exist?)
  • If more than 20% of links are dead, triggers a URL Recovery Re-Search
  • Recovery searches specifically for real, verifiable links from actual web results
  • Remaining dead links apply confidence penalties (>50% dead = −30 confidence)

Delta Verification (Large Score Changes)

If the new Reality Score differs from the previous scan by ≥15 points:

  • Triggers a verification re-scan — runs the entire search again
  • If both scans agree on direction and magnitude, accepts as genuine change (averages the two)
  • If they disagree, uses the more conservative score (closer to previous)

Evidence Persistence (Multi-Scan Memory)

  • Previous scan findings carry forward — "innocent until proven fixed"
  • Not re-finding something = search variance, not proof of improvement
  • Historical evidence expires only after 90 days
  • This prevents "lucky search results" from erasing real problems

Washing Detection

  • If Intent Score > 60 AND Reality Score significantly below Intent: potential washing
  • Classified by type: AI Washing (AI buzzwords, no real adoption), Agile Washing (claims modern practices, evidence of waterfall), Green Washing (ESG narrative, no real sustainable operations)
  • Cannot flag washing if Intent < 40 (low ambition can't be called washing)
  • Re-checked after score verification — if final Reality ≥ Intent, washing flag is removed

Key Outputs

  • Reality Score (0–100): Evidence-based operational maturity
  • Checklist: Per-jury findings with sentiment (positive/negative/neutral), recency, source
  • Evidence Links: Verified URLs with titles, snippets, domains
  • Key People: Executives mentioned in evidence (name, role, relevance)
  • Confidence Score (0–100): How much evidence supports the assessment
  • Washing Flag: Boolean + type if detected

Accuracy Safeguards

  • 5 independent juries ensure diverse evidence (confidence requires 3+ juries with evidence)
  • All evidence URLs verified via HTTP
  • Dead link recovery prevents false low scores from broken links
  • Delta verification re-scans prevent noise from single-run search variance
  • Evidence persistence prevents "lucky" re-scans from hiding real problems
  • Recency weighting ensures stale evidence doesn't dominate
Phase 2b: Financial Health Index Parallel
2b
Financial Health Assessment
Runs parallel to Scanner 2
"How financially healthy is this company? Can they sustain their operations?"

Four Dimensions (Each Scored 0–25)

Revenue Trend (30%) Declining, stable, or growing?
Profitability (40%) Operating margins, net profit
Efficiency (15%) Revenue per employee, operational efficiency
Investment Signal (15%) R&D spending, growth investment, M&A activity

How It Thinks

  • Searches public financial databases, annual reports, employee counts, R&D data
  • Scores each of 4 dimensions independently (0–25 each)
  • Applies a Core Health Gate: If both Revenue and Profitability are weak, the composite score is dampened (floor at 25% of weighted base), regardless of Efficiency or Investment
  • The gate fully opens when Revenue + Profitability reach 60% of their maximum
  • Non-blocking: if Financial search fails, the scan continues with Financial = null

Key Output

  • Financial Index (0–100): Composite health score
  • Sub-scores: Revenue Trend, Profitability, Efficiency, Investment Signal (each 0–25)
  • Financial Findings: Per-dimension evidence with quotes and sources
  • Financial Confidence (0–100): How much data was available
Phase 2c: Self-Analysis Loop Conditional
2c
Score Delta Explanation
Only if score changed ≥15 pts in <7 days
"Why did the scanner produce different results this time? Is it search variance, evidence gaps, prompt sensitivity, or genuine company change?"

Score Drift Guard

If a rescan within 7 days shows a score change ≥15 points, the system first dampens the score (limits change to ±15 max) and then runs a diagnostic analysis to understand why.

Pre-Computed Diagnostics (from actual data, not AI hallucination)

  • Source diversity: How many unique domains in evidence? Is it dominated by one source?
  • Jury coverage: Which juries had evidence, which were empty?
  • Evidence recency: Ratio of recent (<12 months) vs. old findings
  • URL overlap: How many URLs are shared between previous and current scan? Low overlap (<20%) = strong indicator of search variance

Root Cause Classification

  • Search Variance: Different Google results each time (most common; diagnosed by low URL overlap)
  • Evidence Gap: One scan found sources the other missed
  • Prompt Sensitivity: Same evidence, model interpreted differently
  • Genuine Change: Real company change (unlikely within 7 days)

Actionable Suggestions

The system generates improvement recommendations: widen search to parent company, diversify sources, add more language terms, flag low-confidence juries, etc.

All scanner results flow into Synthesis
Phase 3: Synthesis & Cross-Verification Wisdom Layer
3
Independent Score Verification + Narrative Generation
"Do independent scores validate Scanner 1 and Scanner 2? What is the human-readable diagnosis and actionable business pitch?"

Part 1: Independent Score Evaluation (Blind Verification)

A senior AI model (Claude Opus) reads ALL evidence collected by Scanners 1 and 2, but without seeing their scores. It independently evaluates:

  • Reality Score (0–100): Scores each of the 5 Knowledge Base dimensions independently (0–20 each), based only on verified evidence. If no evidence exists for a dimension, scores it 10 (neutral, not generous).
  • Intent Score (0–100): Independently assesses how ambitious the narrative is.

Part 2: Score Cross-Verification

The Synthesis scores are compared against Scanner 1/2 scores to catch disagreements:

Score DeltaActionRationale
≤ 5 points Keep Scanner score Agreement zone — search-grounded score is reliable
6–10 points Blended average (60% Scanner, 40% Synthesis) Mild disagreement — both have valid perspectives
> 10 points Synthesis overrides Scanner Strong disagreement — Synthesis has broader context

Part 3: Narrative Generation

  • Diagnosis / Summary (180–220 words): States the archetype explicitly, maps findings to Knowledge Base dimensions, uses KB terminology (e.g., "Digital Taylorism", "Flow Efficiency", "Pilot Purgatory"), references washing if detected
  • Executive Pitch (90–110 words): Cold-outreach pitch to C-level with exactly 2 concrete proposals derived from the weakest Knowledge Base dimensions. Must feel specific to this company, not a template.

Fallback Chain (Resilience)

If the primary model fails (rate limit, timeout, server error), the system cascades through fallback models:

  • Claude Opus 4.6 (primary) → Gemini 3.1 Pro → Gemini 3 Pro → Sonnet 4.5
  • All three must fail before diagnosis/pitch stay as defaults

Accuracy Safeguards

  • Blind verification: Synthesis scores without seeing Scanner scores
  • Blending logic prevents any single model from dominating
  • Post-verification consistency: if Reality ≥ Intent, washing flag automatically removed
  • Scores recalculated from verified final values (gap, archetype, washing)
  • 3-model fallback chain ensures synthesis completes even under API pressure
Synthesis output flows into Fact-Check
Phase 3b: Fact-Check & Evidence Integrity Gate Agentic AI
3b
Independent Claim Verification Against Live Web Data
"Are the claims in the AI-generated summary actually true? Is the evidence contaminated?"

Core Insight: Evidence Contamination, Not Hallucination

Checking the summary against stored evidence is circular when the evidence itself may be wrong. An independent model with live web search must verify claims against the real world.

Step 1: Heuristic Hallucination Detection (Diagnostic)

A rule-based pre-scan flags obvious red flags in the summary and pitch:

  • Summary references people not found in evidence
  • Summary makes claims with no supporting evidence links
  • Pitch contains generic template language instead of company-specific proposals
  • Confidence score is low but summary sounds certain

Outputs a hallucination risk score (0–100) + specific flags. Diagnostic only — does not block the pipeline.

Step 2: AI Fact-Check (Gemini 3.1 Pro + Google Search Grounding)

An independent AI model with live web search verifies every factual claim in the summary and pitch:

  • Receives the full summary, pitch, evidence links, and company name
  • Uses Google Search to independently verify each factual claim against live web data
  • Classifies each claim as verified (true) or false
  • If false claims found: produces a corrected summary and pitch in the same call (inline correction)

Single-pass guarantee: The fact-check runs exactly once. No recursive loops. The fact-checker fixes what it finds in a single pass.

If Clean (No False Claims)

Original synthesis stands as-is. Pipeline continues to Archetype Classification.

If False Claims Found

  • Evidence Purging: Contaminated evidence sources that supported the false claims are removed from stored data (links, checklist items)
  • Apply Corrected Summary (preferred): Use the corrected summary/pitch produced by the fact-checker in the same call
  • Fallback Re-Synthesis: If the fact-checker did not produce a corrected summary, trigger a separate full re-synthesis from cleaned evidence. The re-synthesis prompt explicitly lists the false claims to avoid.
  • Clear Stale Flags: Remove washing flags that may have been based on now-purged false claims

Key Outputs

  • Verified: Boolean — did the summary pass fact-check?
  • False Claims: List of specific false claims detected (if any)
  • Corrected Summary/Pitch: Fact-checker-produced corrections (if false claims found)
  • Evidence Purged: Count and details of removed contaminated evidence sources
  • Fact-Check Model & Time: Traceability metadata

Accuracy Safeguards

  • Independent model (Gemini) with live web search — avoids circular validation against stored evidence
  • Single-pass guarantee prevents infinite fact-check loops
  • Evidence purging removes the root cause (contaminated sources), not just the symptom (bad text)
  • Inline correction by the same model that found the errors ensures consistency
  • Fallback re-synthesis path ensures correction even if inline correction fails
  • Washing flags cleared after correction to prevent stale false-positive flags
Verified summary flows into Archetype Classification
Phase 4: Archetype Classification
4
Company Archetype Determination
"Given this company's operational reality and financial health, which archetype best describes their position?"

The 2×2 Matrix

Archetype is determined by two axes: Reality Score (operational maturity) and Financial Index (business health):

Thriving

Reality ≥ 50 AND Financial ≥ 50. Modern operations AND healthy business. The gold standard.

Modernizing

Reality ≥ 50 AND Financial < 50. Strong operations but financial pressure. Investing in transformation, returns haven't materialized yet.

Cash Cow

Reality < 50 AND Financial ≥ 50. Profitable but operationally outdated. Making money from legacy advantages. Accumulating technical debt.

At Risk

Reality < 50 AND Financial < 50. Both operations and finances under pressure. Status quo is unsustainable.

If Financial Index is unavailable (search failed), Intent Score is used as a proxy for the financial axis.

Phase 5: Multi-Scan History & Trend Detection
5
Longitudinal Trend Analysis
"How has this company evolved over time? Are issues persistent, resolving, or escalating?"

How It Works

  • Loads up to 3 previous scans from storage (rolling 12-scan history window)
  • Builds a multi-point trend line: [Oldest] → [Mid] → [Recent] → [Now]
  • Classifies trend direction: Improving (Reality up >5), Declining (Reality down >5), or Stable

Persistent Pattern Detection

  • If a finding appears in 2+ scans: high confidence (near-certain pattern)
  • If it appears in 1 scan only: could be search variance
  • If getting worse across scans: escalating (urgent)
  • If absent in recent scans: fading (possibly resolving)

Rescan Mode Verification

For each specific claim from a previous scan, the current scan tries to:

  • Confirm: Found same/similar evidence again
  • Resolve: Found new counter-evidence proving the claim is now fixed
  • Leave unverified: Could not find evidence either way — carried forward as-is (not finding something doesn't mean it's fixed)

What Synthesis Receives

The synthesis prompt includes all trend data, previous summaries, and persistent patterns. It's instructed to note persistent issues, highlight positive trajectories, and avoid treating search variance as genuine improvement.

Phase 6: Statistical Analysis Layer On-Demand
6
Cross-Company Statistical Intelligence
"What patterns emerge across the entire scanned portfolio? How do companies relate to each other statistically?"

Analyses Performed

  • Descriptive Statistics: Median, IQR, mean, std, skew, and distribution shape for each score type (Intent, Reality, Financial, Confidence, Gap)
  • Correlation Analysis: Spearman (primary, non-parametric, robust to outliers) + Kendall's Tau + Pearson (reference). Tests: Is there a monotonic relationship between Reality and Financial scores?
  • Full Correlation Matrix: Intent × Reality × Financial × Confidence (both Spearman and Pearson)
  • Archetype Distribution: Count and percentage of companies in each archetype category
  • Regression Residuals: Which companies deviate most from the expected Reality–Financial relationship? (positive residual = financially stronger than expected; negative = weaker)
  • Domain Breakdown: Average scores by industry vertical
  • Ownership Breakdown: Average scores by ownership type (public, private, state-owned, etc.)
  • Score Distributions: 10-bin histograms for Intent, Reality, and Financial scores
  • Top 10 / Bottom 10: Ranked by Reality Score

Why Non-Parametric Methods Primary?

Spearman and Kendall are used as primary correlation measures because the score distributions are often skewed (not normally distributed). Pearson is included only as a reference point — if Pearson and Spearman differ by >0.15, it indicates the data is significantly non-normal.

Cross-Cutting Verification Mechanisms

Seven Layers of Accuracy Assurance

1. Jury Diversity

Five independent evidence hunts. Confidence only high if 3+ juries return evidence. No single dimension can dominate.

2. Evidence Validation

Every URL verified via HTTP. Dead links trigger recovery. Remaining dead links penalize confidence score.

3. Blind Cross-Verification

Synthesis model scores Reality/Intent independently, without seeing Scanner scores. Disagreements trigger blending or override.

4. AI Fact-Check (Post-Synthesis)

Gemini 3.1 Pro with Google Search independently verifies every claim against live web data. False claims trigger evidence purging and corrected summary. Single-pass guarantee.

5. Score Drift Guard

Limits score changes to ±15 per 7-day window. Large changes trigger self-analysis to diagnose cause (search variance vs. genuine change).

6. Evidence Persistence

"Innocent until proven fixed." Previous findings carry forward for 90 days. Not finding something again = search noise, not improvement.

7. Logic Consistency

Post-verification checks: washing impossible if Reality ≥ Intent. Low ambition can't be washing. Archetype recalculated from final verified scores.

Knowledge Base Grounding
KB
The Foundation: Evolving Knowledge Base

What It Is

Every analysis is grounded in a centralized Knowledge Base that defines the vocabulary, dimensions, archetypes, and patterns the scanner uses. This ensures consistency across all scans and prevents the AI from inventing its own categories.

What It Contains

  • 5 Assessment Dimensions: Operating Model, Strategy, Culture, Service Portfolio, AI Readiness — each with clear good/bad pattern definitions
  • 4 Archetype Definitions: Thriving, Modernizing, Cash Cow, At Risk — with precise classification rules
  • Anti-Pattern Library: "Digital Taylorism", "Pilot Purgatory", "Innovation Theatre", "Flow Efficiency", "Modernization Washing" — named patterns that map to specific evidence signals
  • Scoring Guidelines: How to weight recency, technical debt, and positive signals consistently

How It's Used

  • Scanner 1 uses it for keyword matching (which words are "modern" vs. "legacy")
  • Scanner 2's AI prompts include it to guide evidence search and scoring
  • Synthesis is explicitly instructed: "MUST use KB terminology", "NEVER invent your own categories", "MAP all findings to KB dimensions"
  • No internal codes (TAC-xxx, DIM-xxx) are ever shown to users — always the full concept name
  • Fetched remotely with local cache fallback to ensure availability
